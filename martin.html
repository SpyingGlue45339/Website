Martin Lewis Is Evil

Martin Lewis is the British equivalent of a Russian Oligarch.

Amongst the storied history of our Isle, many figures of great renown have risen above mere ordinary citizens to become icons. This is not to be confused with the spiffing rolling coaster Icon, which is a glorious concoction of metal, albeit located in an area Obi Wan would justifiably call a hive of scum and villainy. Such icons include that guy who built some bridge in Bristol, some woman who wrote about the same love story 50x over (which wasn’t inventive or clever then, nor is it now), that bald guy who wrote plays and who probably didn’t exist, that peruvian immigrant on that podcast, and an over processed ‘meat’ plus pastry slab. Like red hiding in the vents near electrical, however, many imposters somehow find their way into this elite roster. In this two, or maybe three part series, I aim to explore and investigate why these pathetic, ill-born fen rats have snuck onto the pantheon of greatness when so many greats do not.

Why Money Saving Expert is evil:

First of all, even though I would really hope this isn’t a common belief amongst the general public, Martin Lewis does not write anything on Money Saving Expert (hereafter MSE). He hasn’t even owned it for over a decade now, despite some poor pixel-quality image of his face being plastered on the site’s banner for years. The site is very willing to suggest he is deeply involved in it, with his name being the first thing your eyes are drawn to, and of course the aforementioned face, along with the implication there is a money saving expert out there giving you these tips, who is assumed to be Martin, yet of course he plays no role in the writing of it. He may or may not (depending on what the whims of the multi-millionaire class are), get some vague sense of oversight on the company as part of his role sometimes if he wishes. But the very notion that we are getting insights straight from Martin himself needs to be defenestrated before we can continue this discussion.

Discussion might be a bit bold as I’ve recently noticed my affinity for free speech waning, but it’s my website so I can do what I want.

Like any rational person out there you may find yourself wishing to purchase a pink fairy armadillo one day. So, like the sheep of a human you are, you hop on MSE to search for a tasty deal for your tasty purchase, instead of researching it yourself.

The Pink Fairy Armadillo, not to be confused with the Greater Fairy Armadillo nor the Screaming Hairly Armadillo, is a member of the strangest suborder of mammals, sharing it with sloths and anteaters. Males of this suborder have testicles inside the body. Take from this what you will, but bare it in mind.

Let’s refocus onto our delightful employee of MSE, Bartholomew. Bartholomew is facing a difficult choice. Having looked into the providers, he’s come up with this spreadsheet.

Provider Referral Link Customer Discount
Pangolin Inc 4.5% 20% off
Xany Xenarthra 1% 50% off

Which should he post onto MSE?

Now, on the one hand Xany Xenarthra are offering a rather spiffing deal, aren’t they, especially given their favourable phylogenetic description. 50% off. I could buy two of those little pink suckers for the price of one. But… Bartholomew imagines the expected revenue from this deal. He then pictures the vulnerable flock directed towards Pangolin Inc’s deal, and the wave of cash that would bring in, along with his performance bonus for this quarter, then makes his mind up.

The best deal for the customer has not been selected. In fact, it is easy to argue the best deal for the customer is consistently selected against. You would imagine the money the company pays to MSE would be subtracted from the discount they can offer the public. So in a very real sense, MSE is stealing from poor companies just trying to help the public out, as well as the consumer directly by taking money off their discount. They’re the classic parasitic middleman, helping no-one and harming everyone.

An aura-farming representation of Martin Lewis' true form.

But the trouble for Bartholomew does not end there. It turns out that, due to a lack of research, he has signposted thousands of people towards buying not armadillos, but pangolins instead, which have a far more earthy and darker meat. This disastrous circumstance arose because Bartholomew has no accountability (and he still gets the referral commission anyways). MSE doesn’t pretend to be financial advice. There are no Government standards enforcing rules upon this scam. It’s a glorified blog. You’re left to the whims of whatever scummy actions the company in question will pull, and MSE is left with the moral responsibility for this event, yet has no legal repercussions.

But that story doesn’t happen to you, dearest reader. When you enter that slaughterhouse of enterprise and independence called MSE, you find a new link, this time for

Provider Referral Link Customer Discount
Bombadillo Armadillo 10% 10% off

You see, just two hours seven minutes four seconds and seven arcseconds previously Bartholomew had been sent an email by this shell company (lol) advertising a new deal for customers. Following the reasoning above, Bartholomew immediately places it in the site, seeing the advantage for him. What’s the issue here, he’s still giving customers 10% off?

Well, it doesn’t take the sharpest tool in the shed to notice this may be seen as a form of bribery, given the expected direct revenue for MSE. This is a common process at MSE. Company sends link; MSE advertises. MSE is essentially acting as a glorified marketing tool for companies, whilst operating under the guise of being some sort of consumer help-site. In every dimension, the consumers globally lose out, and only MSE profits from this interaction.

And then you, having lost out to Martin and his gang, then finally tuck in to your delightful pork-like feast, unwittingly starting a whole new pandemic.

Those are the consequences of Martin Lewis.

It’s no coincidence that he was around at the last pandemic.

Why the Perception of MoneySavingExpert is evil:

When Andrew Burnham’s first budget was released, you would have expected the British public to be the ones to find out first.

I may be of some shock to find out that Martin Lewis had a meeting with the Chancellor the morning of the release to receive a special briefing of the budget (probably finding time to flog a few scaly mammals to the willing Number Eleven flock, too).

Martin Lewis got a personalised budget presentation before the British public.

Just think about how absurd it would be if Rupert Murdoch was summoned to receive a briefing of the latest Government policies before the British public: there’d be uproar!

Martin’s little outing to the centre of Government, contrastingly, was far less unreasonable: he had been drafted in to consult on the budget in the first place, so naturally he should be able to see the fruits of his labour. Although this does raise the even bigger issue of, say, Murdoch being invited to write policy.

But Martin Lewis gets a pass?

Now, dear reader, I hear you shouting at the screen that Martin Lewis is an expert and Murdoch is just some shady businessman. Martin is the money saving expert!

I counter that by stating that Martin Lewis is an expert in diddly squat. He has the economics education of a loony llama with listeria. For Martin Lewis has a Law and Government Bsc, not a Law degree mind you, and that’s all his relevant education. The British public are supposed to reject the insights of real economics experts, such as the kind you would hope would be consulted on the budget, in favour of this pretentious pratt. He’s not even qualified to be a Financial Advisor!

A Llama of colour identifying as an Alpaca.

Even worse than the actual Governmental power he seems to be able to wield is his sway over the general public. People blindly listen to him and trust him, partially because they have been bribed by the website he presides over. Martin Lewis has no foundation enabling him to be seen as any source of authority for finances, and yet the media wheel him out like a clown on a hospital bed every time they want to claim to be in-touch and helping ordinary people, even though Martin Lewis is about as in-touch as a tapir in the troposphere. Lewis is an establishment man through and through, claiming to learn his great personal finance knowledge working for the BBC, an organisation that is famously full of grounded, sincere and well-meaning individuals, just like the man who made that statue on their building (for legal reasons this is a joke). He is another of the establishment-rebellion types who fall firmly within the state’s own paradigm, offering meaningless, trivial changes working inside the system we appear to be stuck in. Which works well as a metaphor for Andy Burnham and his namby-pamby do-nothing kick-the-can-down-the-road budget, too, as a matter of fact, which is about as effective and meaningful as putting a male and female panda in a cage and expecting them to breed.

Conclusion:

We can see that Martin Lewis is an evil, evil man. He makes poor companies lose money. He makes you lose money. He is unaccountable and unqualified for the meagre post of website-owner and yet he is elevated beyond his station in an establishment plot into a Government consultant and the voice of all wisdom about anything money-related. He is despicable and we deserve the right to never have to hear this scammer ever again.
